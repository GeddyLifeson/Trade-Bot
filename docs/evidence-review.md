# Build on factors, trend and premiums

The published evidence supports a small set of tactics for this bot, and none of them earns 10% a year alone. The strongest are equity momentum, quality and value as a stock-picking core; cross-asset trend following run through ETFs; put-writing to collect the volatility risk premium; and a small crypto trend sleeve. These have long histories, replicate across markets, and trade slowly enough to survive costs. Most event-driven signals, calendar effects and machine-learning forecasts either faded after publication, live in microcaps a bot can't trade cheaply, or need data you don't have for free. Plan on any published edge shrinking by about half once you trade it ([McLean & Pontiff](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/cfa-digest/2016/06/does-academic-research-destroy-stock-return-predictability-digest-summary)). The 10% target is also the long-run return of the US stock market itself: **9.7% a year nominal from 1900 to 2024** ([UBS Yearbook 2025](https://elements.visualcapitalist.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/GIRY2025-summary-public.pdf)). So the bot reaches 10% mainly by holding equity-like risk and adding a few points of tilt, and it will miss in bad years. Retail track records make the same point. In Taiwan, **fewer than 1% of day traders** were reliably profitable after costs ([Barber, Lee, Liu & Odean](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/Day%20Trading%20Skill%20110523.pdf)). The recommended blend is below, with the master verdict table and the backtest rules that keep it honest.

"Inference" marks the researchers' or my own judgment.

## The 10% target equals the market, so the bot must hold risk

Start with the benchmark. US stocks returned **9.7% a year nominal over 1900-2024**. Long Treasuries returned 4.6% and T-bills 3.4% ([UBS Yearbook 2025](https://elements.visualcapitalist.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/GIRY2025-summary-public.pdf)). Since 2000, global stocks returned only **3.5% a year real**, roughly 6% nominal. The same source lists long recoveries. The US market took about 15.5 years to recover from 1929, about 7.5 years from the 2000 peak, and about 4 years from the 2009 low. Factor premiums can stay negative for whole decades. A target of 10% a year at any account size therefore means matching a historically strong market, after costs, with equity-sized drawdowns. A bot can't promise that as a steady yearly yield.

Published edges shrink in live use. McLean and Pontiff studied 97 return predictors. Returns were **26% lower out of sample and 58% lower after publication** ([CFA Digest summary](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/cfa-digest/2016/06/does-academic-research-destroy-stock-return-predictability-digest-summary)). Their raw monthly means fell from 0.582% in sample to 0.264% after publication. Signals with bigger in-sample returns decayed more. So did signals built only from price and volume data, and signals in liquid, large stocks ([McLean & Pontiff PDF](https://Www.Gwern.net/doc/economics/2016-mclean.pdf)). Jensen, Kelly and Pedersen found **average alpha fell from 0.49% to 0.26% a month out of sample**, about a 47% drop ([JKP publisher PDF](https://research-api.cbs.dk/ws/portalfiles/portal/95651880/theis_ingerslev_jensen_et_al_is_there_a_replication_crisis_in_finance_publishersversion.pdf)).

Whether a factor "replicates" depends on how you build it. Hou, Xue and Zhang tested 452 anomalies with value weights and NYSE size breakpoints, which removes the microcap effect. **65% failed a t-stat of 1.96, 82% failed at 2.78, and 96% of "trading friction" anomalies failed** ([HXZ](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v33y2020i5p2019-2133..html)). Chen and Zimmermann reproduced 98% of clearly significant predictors using the original methods ([Fed FEDS](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/open-source-cross-sectional-asset-pricing.htm)). JKP put the US replication rate at 82.4% on CAPM alpha ([JKP PDF](https://research-api.cbs.dk/ws/portalfiles/portal/95651880/theis_ingerslev_jensen_et_al_is_there_a_replication_crisis_in_finance_publishersversion.pdf)). A secondary summary rounds this to 85% ([Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/is-there-a-replication-crisis-in-finance/)). Ten of 13 factor themes replicate above 75%. **Seasonality, leverage and size do not.** For a 20-stock book of liquid names, the HXZ value-weighted numbers are the right guide. Costs set a second filter. Novy-Marx and Velikov found few strategies with **more than 50% one-way monthly turnover** survive costs. A buy/hold rank band was the best simple fix ([NBER w20721](https://nber.org/papers/w20721)).

Retail outcomes set the base rate. In Taiwan, about 360,000 people day traded in an average year. **Only 13-15% made money net of fees in a given year, and under 1% could be predicted to keep doing so** ([Barber et al.](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/Day%20Trading%20Skill%20110523.pdf)). In Brazil, **97% of persistent futures day traders lost money**, and only 0.4% earned more than a bank teller's starting wage ([Chague, De-Losso & Giovannetti](https://ideas.repec.org/p/fgv/eesptd/525.html)). US retail options traders **lost about $2.1bn from November 2019 to June 2021**. About $6.5bn of their costs were spreads, against $950m in commissions. Quoted spreads on the short-dated options retail favored averaged 12.6% ([Bryzgalova, Pavlova & Sikorskaya](https://repec.cepr.org/repec/cpr/ceprdp/DP17688.pdf)). I found no rigorous study of retail algo-bot outcomes. That is an open gap. The lesson for this bot is that costs and turnover decide outcomes. Trade slowly, and lean on edges that rest on a risk premium more than on prediction.

## Momentum, quality and value form the stock core

**Momentum** is the best stock signal for this system. It needs only daily prices, and it works across eight markets and asset classes, with a negative correlation to value ([Asness, Moskowitz & Pedersen](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/360210)). Its weakness is crashes. Momentum loses badly in rebounds after bear markets. A dynamic version that forecasts its mean and variance **roughly doubles the alpha and Sharpe ratio** of plain momentum ([Daniel & Moskowitz](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20439)). Residual momentum ranks stocks on returns after stripping out Fama-French factors. Over 1926-2009 it had a **gross Sharpe of 0.90 against 0.45 for plain momentum**, with 12.5% volatility against 22.7%. In 2000-2009 it made 4.7% a year while plain momentum lost 8.5% ([CXO summary of Blitz, Huij & Martens](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/momentum-investing/stripping-risks-from-a-stock-momentum-strategy/)). These are equal-weighted, long-short, gross figures, so expect less in a long-only top-20 book. The classic effect size is about 1% a month top-minus-bottom decile [memory]. Monthly turnover runs about 30-40% [memory], under the 50% cost threshold. Factor returns for the residual version are free from the Ken French library. A long-only winners book is close to a high-beta growth portfolio, so pair it with a trend or volatility control (inference).

**Quality and profitability** pair naturally with value. Gross profits to assets predicts returns about as well as book-to-market, and it works among the largest, most liquid stocks ([Novy-Marx](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v108y2013i1p1-28.html)). Quality-minus-junk earns significant risk-adjusted returns in the US and 24 other countries ([Asness, Frazzini & Pedersen](https://ideas.repec.org/a/spr/reaccs/v24y2019i1d10.1007_s11142-018-9470-2.html)). The value-weighted GP/A spread was about 4% a year [memory], and quality tends to hold up in crashes [memory]. JKP find profitability partly absorbed by other factors, so use it inside a composite. Turnover is very low.

**Value** earns its place as a hedge for momentum. In JKP's results its weight rises once other factors are controlled for ([Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/is-there-a-replication-crisis-in-finance/)). It had a deep slump in 2018-2020, driven by widening valuation spreads, and recovered from November 2020. Long-only value lagged in 2023 because of the largest growth stocks ([Robeco](https://www.robeco.com/en-latam/insights/2024/01/value-investing-the-reports-of-my-death-have-been-greatly-exaggerated)). French's HML was near zero or negative from 2007 to 2020 [memory]. Use a composite of earnings, book and cash-flow yields combined with profitability. Quality and value both need fundamentals Alpaca doesn't supply. SEC EDGAR XBRL is the free source.

The other equity signals are smaller tools. **Industry momentum** explains much of stock momentum and stays profitable after controls ([Moskowitz & Grinblatt](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v54y1999i4p1249-1290.html)), and sector ETFs make it cheap to trade. **Low volatility** replicates, but the betting-against-beta long-short had a Sharpe of 0.42 with a **-61.5% max drawdown**. Its returns lean on microcaps, and Quantpedia's out-of-sample test is slightly negative ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/betting-against-beta-factor-in-stocks)). Long-only low-vol gave about market returns at 70-80% of market volatility [memory], so it lowers risk but adds no return. **Short interest** predicts weak returns for heavily shorted stocks ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/short-interest-effect-long-only-version)). Aggregate short interest is "arguably the strongest known predictor of aggregate stock returns" ([WashU](https://source.washu.edu/2015/05/why-investors-should-listen-to-short-sellers-yes-short-sellers/)). Quantpedia's 26.8% a year long-only figure is gross, equal-weighted and pre-2005, so use short interest as a screen. **Turn-of-month** put all the market's excess return from 1987 to 2005 in a four-day window, and it holds in 30 of 34 countries ([CXO summary of Xu & McConnell](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/calendar-effects/the-turn-of-the-month-effect/)). No post-2005 test was opened, and the window covers only about 18% of days, so it serves to schedule rebalances. **Short-term reversal** fails cost tests outside large caps. One 2012 study found 30-50 bps a week net in large caps with turnover controls ([de Groot, Huij & Zhou](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jbfina/v36y2012i2p371-382.html)), which is optimistic for 2026. Use it only to time entries after a pullback. **Heston-Sadka seasonality** falls in JKP's failing theme. **Customer-supplier momentum** fell from about 1.3% to 0.6% a month value-weighted after 2005 and lost significance ([Pinchuk](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2301.11394)). **Analyst revisions** have only 1990s evidence ([Chan, Jegadeesh & Lakonishok](https://www.nber.org/papers/w5375)) and need a paid estimates feed. The January and pre-holiday effects have largely faded [memory].

## Insider buying survives while most event signals have faded

**Opportunistic insider purchases** are the best free event signal. Cohen, Malloy and Pomorski separated routine insider trades, made in the same month each year, from the rest. **Opportunistic trades earned 82 bps a month value-weighted from 1986 to 2007. Routine trades earned about zero** ([NBER Digest](https://www.nber.org/digest/apr11/decoding-inside-information)). Form 4 filings are free on EDGAR and due within two business days [memory]. The sample ends in 2007, the signal is widely known, and the effect is stronger in small firms. Expect about half the published alpha, and apply a liquidity floor (inference). It works as a ranking boost inside the stock sleeve.

**LLM news sentiment** shows real predictive power, but the tradable version for this bot is unclear. GPT-4 headline scores predict next-day drift, "especially for small stocks and negative news." The returns shrink as LLM adoption rises ([Lopez-Lira & Tang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07619v6)). One preprint reports a **net Sharpe of 2.85** out of sample from June 2024 to January 2026 at 5 bps cost ([Kirtac](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.23703)). That is a daily-rebalanced, value-weighted long-short on a premium Refinitiv feed. It is not peer reviewed or independently replicated, and it doesn't model latency. A long-only, next-open, 20-name book on Alpaca's free news would capture a fraction of that. The strongest part of the effect is negative news, so the safe use is a filter: don't buy, and consider exiting, names with sharply negative scored news or 8-K text (inference). Look-ahead bias is the big hazard. A model can remember outcomes from its training data [memory, Glasserman & Lin]. Test only on news dated after the model's training cutoff.

**13D activist filings** brought about 7% abnormal returns around filing [memory, Brav et al.], much of it earned by the filing date. The deadline shrank to five business days in 2024 [memory]. Detection is free and same-day, but events in liquid names are few. Treat it as a small ranking boost. **Options-implied signals** are partly alive. A 2026 preprint on 2015-2026 data finds the call-minus-put implied-volatility spread (t=4.2) and risk-neutral skewness (t=-11.7) still predict 21-day returns, while the volatility smirk faded to insignificance ([Li & Wang](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.26115)). It reports no portfolio returns or costs, and historical option surfaces aren't free. Start logging option chains daily now, and revisit in a year or two.

The rest should be dropped or used only as avoidance rules. **PEAD vanished in non-microcap stocks after 2001-2006.** Excluding microcaps, the 2001-2024 t-stat is 1.43 ([UCLA Anderson Review](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/is-post-earnings-announcement-drift-a-thing-again/)). It survives only as a rule to avoid buying after a large negative surprise. **The S&P 500 inclusion effect went from +7.4% in the 1990s to under 1% and insignificant in the 2010s.** Deletions went from -16.1% to -0.6% ([Alpha Architect on Greenwood & Sammon](https://alphaarchitect.com/disappearing-index-effect/)). **Congressional portfolios underperformed a passive index by about 2.8% a year in 2004-2008**, with "no evidence of informed trading" ([Eggers & Hainmueller](https://www.mit.edu/~jhainm/Paper/CL.pdf)). Buyback announcements swing by period: 3-year excess returns were 20.1% in 1994-2001, 2.4% and insignificant in 2002-2006, and 13.9% in 2007-2014 ([Lee, Park & Pearson](https://www.business.kaist.ac.kr/faculty/inmoo/Articles/RepurchasesHaveChanged_June2019_final.pdf)). Merger arbitrage spreads have shrunk, and part of the decline looks permanent ([Analysis Group](https://www.analysisgroup.com/Insights/ag-feature/merger-arbitrage-spread---still-shrinking-/)). With T-bills paying about 4-5%, the excess is thin (inference), and a deal break costs 20-40% on one position. A 2024 study of 13F cloning sits behind a paywall, so its findings are unverified ([CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/miscellaneous/form-13f-clone-portfolio-performance/)). Spin-off outperformance [memory], earnings-call tone [memory] and IPO lockup declines of about 1-2% [memory] have no verified recent evidence. Lockup dates do support one cheap avoidance rule: don't hold a recent IPO into its lockup expiry.

## Trend, put-writing and crypto give the return diversification

**Cross-asset trend following** has the longest record on the list. Hurst, Ooi and Pedersen take data back to 1880 and describe the strategy as "consistently profitable over the next 110 years" ([AQR](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/A-Century-of-Evidence-on-Trend-Following-Investing)). Its live record shows both sides. The SG Trend Index made **+20.9% in 2008 and +27.4% in 2022** while world stocks fell. It then **lost 20.4% from May 2024 to May 2025** while stocks rose 18.3%. It has had 16 drawdowns over 10% since 2000 ([Cambridge Associates](https://www.cambridgeassociates.com/insight/does-trend-followings-recent-struggle-signal-that-the-strategy-is-structurally-broken/)). Backtests show a Sharpe above 1 [memory], but plan for 0.3-0.6 live (inference). Faber's 200-day-average rule is the simplest version. Out of sample from 2006 to March 2025 it returned **6.05% a year with a 0.68 Sharpe and an 11.7% max drawdown**. Results changed meaningfully with the rebalance day, so split the sleeve into tranches that rebalance on different days ([Concretum](https://concretumgroup.com/global-tactical-asset-allocation/); [CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/strategic-allocation/extension-of-a-quantitative-approach-to-tactical-asset-allocation)). The sleeve earns its place by paying off in stock crashes. It won't hit 10% alone. Dual momentum is a variant of the same idea, and it whipsawed after publication [memory]. Risk parity needs leverage to reach equity-like returns, and it was hit hard in 2022 [memory]. Business-cycle sector rotation added little even with perfect knowledge of the cycle [memory].

**Volatility targeting** controls risk but adds no return. Cederburg and co-authors tested 103 strategies. The real-time volatility-managed versions **did not beat the unmanaged ones, and earned lower Sharpe ratios**, because the underlying regressions were unstable ([Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/does-portfolio-timing-based-on-volatility-signals-outperform-buy-and-hold/)). Momentum held up best. Use a 10-12% portfolio volatility target to cut the left tail, especially on the momentum sleeve, and don't count it toward the 10%. Fractional Kelly works the same way as a cap. Half-Kelly gives about 75% of full-Kelly growth at half the volatility [memory]. With live Sharpe near 0.3-0.6, that points to about 1x gross exposure (inference). HMM regime models mostly repeat what a 200-day filter does, with extra overfitting risk [memory].

**Put-writing** harvests a real premium. Implied volatility on the Russell 2000 exceeded realized by about **3.3 points on average from 2001 to 2019** ([Cboe on Wilshire](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/new-wilshire-paper-uncovers-where-to-find-enhanced-risk-adjusted-returns)). The CBOE PUT index **returned 10.1% a year at 10.1% volatility from 1986 to 2015, against 9.8% at 15.3% for the S&P 500**. In 2006-2015 its max drawdown was -33% against -51% ([CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/equity-options/performance-of-cboe-putwrite-indexes)). These are gross index figures. ETFs tracking the covered-call index trailed the index substantially. Covered calls carry an extra equity bet that behaves like a reversal strategy and isn't paid for ([Israelov](https://www.aqr.com/insights/research/journal-article/covered-calls-uncovered)), so prefer cash-secured puts. No data after 2019 was verified. Expect about 6-9% a year, with a short-volatility tail (inference). At $100k, sizing is lumpy: one SPY put ties up $50-60k of collateral (inference). Use monthly, roughly 30-delta puts on lower-priced ETFs, and stay out of short-dated contracts, where retail spreads averaged 12.6%.

**Crypto trend** is the one sleeve with real upside, and also the most volatile. In 2011-2018 Bitcoin data, a one-standard-deviation weekly gain predicted **about 3.2-3.7% further gains over each of the next three weeks**. The effect roughly halved after 2013, to a 4.35-point weekly quintile spread ([Liu & Tsyvinski](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w24877/w24877.pdf)). No 2019-2026 out-of-sample test was opened, and the market is far more institutional now that spot ETFs exist. Run BTC and ETH long-or-flat on a 1-4 week trend rule, evaluate weekly, and use bands, since Alpaca's taker fees of about 0.15-0.25% [memory] punish frequent switching. Cap the sleeve at 5-15% of total risk.

## Machine-learning forecasts add little on liquid names

Cross-sectional machine learning looks strong in academic samples, but most of its profit sits where a retail bot can't collect it. Gu, Kelly and Xiu reported a monthly out-of-sample R² of about 0.3-0.4% and a value-weighted long-short Sharpe of about 1.3-1.4, gross [memory]. Avramov, Cheng and Metzker found the gains come mainly from hard-to-arbitrage stocks. Profits are **"considerably attenuated" after excluding microcaps, distressed firms or high-volatility periods**, and shrink further after costs because of high turnover ([SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3450322)). The long side and recent years held up better. Time-series foundation models did worse. A June 2026 test of Chronos, TimesFM, Moirai and TimeGPT on five large stocks found only **2 of the model-stock cases significantly beat a random walk**. The authors say the models are not reliable alpha engines ([Noguer i Alonso & Pereira Franklin](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.27100)). Pairs trading earned up to 11% a year excess in 1962-2002 ([Alpha Architect on GGR](https://alphaarchitect.com/pairs-trading-kicks-butt/)). Later studies found it largely gone after costs and borrow fees [memory]. The useful role for ML here is narrow: volatility forecasts, or combining a handful of known signals, under strict cross-validation. The LLM news filter covered above is the one AI tool with live evidence behind it.

## A four-sleeve blend fits inside 20 positions

The blend spreads risk across sources that fail at different times. Momentum suffers in rebounds, value in growth manias, trend in choppy rallies, put-writing in crashes. The weights below are my judgment from the evidence. No source tested this exact mix.

| Sleeve | Capital | Positions | Rules |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stock composite: residual momentum + quality + value | 50% | 12 | Rank the S&P 500 or Russell 1000 on an equal-weight blend of the three scores. Insider and 13D signals add a rank boost. Rebalance monthly near turn-of-month. |
| Cross-asset trend / GTAA | 30% | 5 | SPY, EFA, IEF/TLT, GLD, DBC, VNQ class ETFs. Hold each only above its 200-day average, else T-bills (BIL/SGOV). Split into weekly tranches. |
| Cash-secured puts | 12% | 1-2 | Monthly, about 30-delta, on liquid lower-priced ETFs. Collateral sits in T-bills. |
| Crypto trend | 5-8% | 1-2 | BTC and ETH, long only when the 1-4 week trend is up. Weekly checks with bands. Sized to stay under about 15% of total risk. |

**The rotation rule** keeps turnover down. A stock enters when it ranks in the top 12 of the composite. It leaves only when it falls out of the top 30, or a filter fires. That band is the cost fix Novy-Marx and Velikov found most effective ([NBER w20721](https://nber.org/papers/w20721)). A new candidate replaces the weakest holding only when it beats that holding's score by a set margin. Every position also gets a minimum hold of about one month. Filters can force an exit at any time: sharply negative LLM-scored news or 8-K text, a large earnings miss, top-decile short interest, or an upcoming lockup expiry. They never force a buy.

**Overlays sit on top.** Target 10-12% annual portfolio volatility from 20-60 day realized volatility, and cap gross exposure at 1.0x. When SPY is below its 200-day average, cut the stock sleeve toward half and park the rest in T-bills (inference). Cap any single stock at about 6% of capital.

**What to expect.** The allocation note puts a blend like this at about **7-10% gross with 8-12% volatility** (inference). The stock sleeve should track the market plus or minus a few points; the equity note expects a few percent of tilt over the market at best. Trend adds 4-8% with crash protection, puts 6-9%, and crypto adds lumpy upside. Hitting 10% net in a given year depends mostly on the stock market. With only 12-20 names, tracking error against the index is large, about 8-12% a year [memory], so a 2-4% factor edge is noise over one to three years. Paper trading for a few months can't prove or disprove the edge.

## Backtests need a trial count, purged splits and real costs

**Count every variant you test.** The deflated Sharpe ratio corrects a backtest Sharpe for the number of strategies tried and for fat tails ([Bailey & López de Prado](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551)). Accept a strategy only if its DSR is at least 0.95. With one trial and an annual Sharpe of 1, that takes about three years of daily returns ([Wikipedia: DSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deflated_Sharpe_ratio)). The probability of backtest overfitting, estimated by combinatorially symmetric cross-validation, checks how often the best in-sample setup lands below median out of sample. Its authors find standard hold-out tests "unreliable and inaccurate" for investment backtests ([Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2326253)). No source opened sets a PBO cutoff. Rejecting above about 0.2-0.5 is a judgment call. Any new signal you discover yourself should clear **a t-stat above 3.0** ([Harvey, Liu & Zhu](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20592)).

**Use purged, embargoed splits.** Purging removes training data whose label window overlaps the test window. An embargo drops data right after each test fold. Combinatorial purged CV yields a distribution of Sharpe ratios and drawdowns where walk-forward yields one path. For example, 6 groups with 2 test groups give 15 splits and 5 paths ([Wikipedia: purged CV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purged_cross-validation)). Keep walk-forward as a final check.

**Fix the data and cost model before trusting any statistic.** Use a universe that includes delisted stocks, point-in-time fundamentals with realistic reporting lags, and signals timestamped before the order. For LLM signals, test only on news after the model's training cutoff. Fill orders at the far side of the quote, not at mid. Use 5-10 bps per side for liquid stocks (equity note, inference), and assume 10% or more on short-dated options ([BPS](https://repec.cepr.org/repec/cpr/ceprdp/DP17688.pdf)). Haircut any published premium by 50-60% before costs. Rebalance-date luck is real, so test several start days or tranche the rebalances ([Concretum](https://concretumgroup.com/global-tactical-asset-allocation/)). Treat any backtest showing a Sharpe above 1, or above 15% a year with a Sharpe above 1.5, as overfit until proven otherwise (inference). Once live, set a kill switch: shrink or stop a sleeve when its live Sharpe stays well below the deflated backtest Sharpe.

## Conclusion

The evidence says this bot should run a few slow, well-known premiums cheaply and steadily. The tactics that survive replication, decay and costs are the ones academics and big funds have known about for decades. The newest-looking ideas, such as foundation models, congressional trades and index events, mostly fail. LLM news scoring is the exception worth watching. Its best use for now is keeping the bot out of bad news, while one unreplicated preprint carries most of the case for it as a return engine.

Set the 10% target as a long-run average with equity-like swings, and judge the bot against SPY and a simple 60/40 over full cycles. The useful near-term work is plumbing: free EDGAR fundamentals and Form 4 parsing, a trial log that feeds the deflated Sharpe test, and a daily options-chain archive, so the options signals can be tested in a year or two. The weakest links in this review are the [memory] figures and the missing post-2019 data for puts and crypto trend. Verify those before committing real money.
